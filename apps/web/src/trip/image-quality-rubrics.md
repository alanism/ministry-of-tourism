# MoT Image Quality Rubrics

Two distinct, style-specific rubrics for comparing an illustration with the user's source photo and an approved style reference. Each rubric contains **20 criteria scored on five levels, 1–5**, for a **raw total of 20–100, reported /100**. These are judging templates, not scores for an image already inspected.

**Acceptance target: at least 80/100 and every critical gate passed.** A high total cannot compensate for a critical failure.

## Before scoring

View the **source photo, approved style reference, and candidate together**. Establish:

- **Photo locks:** subjects, pose/viewpoint, important clothing/buildings, spatial relationships, and crop.
- **Permitted changes:** elongation, simplification, omitted clutter, palette changes, and background treatment.
- **Style locks:** line behavior, shading, texture, color placement, and any MoT block.
- **Delivery requirements:** size, format, background, and whether actual transparency is required.

A **minor error** is localized and does not change recognition, geometry, garment design, or visual hierarchy. A **major error** changes one of those things. Intentional, approved stylization is not an error.

Score only visible evidence. For obscured features, reward preserving that uncertainty rather than inventing details. For an absent element, assess whether its absence is correctly preserved. Record a short evidence note for each criterion so scores can be checked and revisions can target specific defects.

### Fashion-specific critical gates

Fail approval regardless of total if:

- A required person is added, removed, or materially replaced.
- A locked pose, defining outfit, or key accessory is changed.
- Severe visible anatomy defects remain.
- The result uses an incompatible medium or misses the essential approved editorial style.
- A shared delivery gate below fails.

### City-specific critical gates

Fail approval regardless of total if:

- The main building, landmark, or street arrangement is materially replaced.
- A locked viewpoint or defining architectural feature is changed.
- Perspective defects make the principal structure visibly impossible.
- The required MoT block is absent or fundamentally wrong.
- The output becomes an incompatible rendering style rather than the approved ink treatment.
- A shared delivery gate below fails.

## Shared delivery gates

Check separately from the artistic score:

- Required dimensions, file type, and usable resolution are met.
- No unauthorized text, signature, watermark, logo, or decorative element appears. Preserve source signage only as specified.
- Required content is not accidentally cropped or obscured.
- **If transparency is required, inspect the actual alpha channel. A white background or checkerboard painted into the image is not transparency.** Composite onto both light and dark backgrounds to check edges.
- Only require exact MoT typography, logo geometry, or block placement when the approved reference or brief actually specifies it. Do not invent brand rules.

## Practical scoring and bounded revision loop

1. **Lock the brief.** Record the required photo details, permitted stylization, approved reference, and delivery requirements.
2. **Inspect at two scales.** Judge composition at fit-to-screen; inspect anatomy, architecture, edges, and artifacts at 100%.
3. **Apply the gates first.** Record each as pass/fail and identify the visible evidence.
4. **Score all 20 rows.** Record a score plus specific evidence for every row, and a required fix for each defect. Do not award style points simply because an image is attractive.
5. **Check the target.** Accept when the total is **at least 80/100 and every critical gate passes**. Report any remaining noncritical weaknesses honestly. Do not silently raise the target to 90 or add a minimum score per criterion.
6. **Choose the next edit if needed.** Fix critical failures first, then the two or three most consequential low-scoring criteria. Explicitly preserve successful features.
7. **Regenerate or edit and fully rescore.** Improvements can introduce regressions elsewhere. Retain the best complete candidate and its scorecard.
8. **Bound the retries.** By default, allow **one initial candidate plus at most two revision passes, three candidates total**. Stop early when the acceptance target is met. If the cap is reached, stop, present the best candidate with its score and unresolved failures, and ask before further attempts. Never claim success merely because the retry limit was reached.

## Limits and calibration

- These totals are structured judgments, not percentages of objective correctness or guarantees of identity preservation.
- A 5 means excellent execution of the **approved transformation**, not photographic duplication or maximum detail.
- Hidden or unreadable source features cannot be verified; do not reward invented certainty.
- Distinguish deliberate sketch irregularity from structural errors.
- Keep photo fidelity and style fidelity separate: an elegant image can still fail the source, and an accurate tracing can still fail the style.
- If judges differ by more than one point on a row, compare the same visible evidence and resolve the anchor interpretation before averaging.
- If no approved reference is available, source fidelity can be scored, but claims of a verified MoT style match must remain provisional. If required delivery properties cannot be inspected, mark them unverified rather than passed.
